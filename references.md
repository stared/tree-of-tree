# References — *The Tree of "Tree"*

Every etymological claim in this project is traced to a source below. The project
deliberately distinguishes **secure** descent (agreed across sources) from
**disputed** links (flagged in the visualization with a dashed line and a "?"),
and this file records *who* disputes what.

Two reconstruction conventions appear throughout and are **not** a real
disagreement, just different notations:

- **etymonline** labels the root **`*deru-`** "be firm, solid, steadfast," with
  specialized senses "wood, tree" and many suffixed variants (`*drew-o-`,
  `*dru-`, `*dru-ko-`, `*derw-`, `*dreu-`).
- **Wiktionary** labels the headword **`*dóru`** (neuter noun) "tree; wood; spear,"
  an athematic proterokinetic noun with oblique stem `*drew-` and collective
  `*dru-`, and notes that *some* derive it from an adjective `*deru-/*drew-`
  "hard, firm, solid."

This project writes the root as **`*deru- / *dóru-`** to hold both.

## §3 — Disputed links (shown dashed + "?" in the visualization)

### D1. Latin *dūrus* "hard" ← *deru-*  — **disputed**
- **For (AHD/Watkins, etymonline):** *dūrus* and its family (*endure, durable, dour, obdurate, duress, duramen, dura mater*…) derive from a suffixed variant of *deru-* "be firm, solid." — `[R24]`,`[R2]`,`[R25]`.
- **Against (de Vaan 2008:184, Wiktionary):** preferred etymology is PIE *duh₂-ró-s* "long" ← *dweh₂-* "far, long" (cognate Greek *dērós* "long," Sanskrit *dūrá* "far"); the *deru-* link is demoted to an "alternative." — `[R26]`,`[R27]` — "there are semantic problems if the change 'long' > 'enduring' is not accepted."
- **Note:** etymonline and AHD share the **Watkins** framework, so they are *one* tradition, not two independent confirmations.

### D2. *druid* = "oak-knower" (*deru-* "oak" + *weid-* "know") — **disputed**
- **For (traditional; Pliny):** *dru-* = "oak" (← *dóru*); "they who know the oak." — `[R48]` (hedged with "perhaps"), `[R50]`.
- **Against (post-1960s scholarship, per Wiktionary):** the oak link is "doubtful both on phonological and historical grounds"; first element instead from *drew-* "firm, solid" → "firm/great knower, great sage." — `[R49]`. Either way the first element lies in the *deru-/*drew-* complex; only the **"oak"** gloss is contested.

### D3. Russian *zdorov* / Polish *zdrowy* "healthy" = "of good wood" — **disputed**
- **For ("tree/wood" reading):** PSlav *sъdorvъ* = *sъ-* "good" + *-dorv-* (← *dóru* "tree, wood") → "of sound wood" → "healthy." — `[R63]`.
- **Against (Meillet; Derksen):** second element from *dʰer-* "to support, hold" (PIE *h₁su-dʰor-wo-*), needed to satisfy Winter's law; Wiktionary: "The exact origin of the second component *dorv- is uncertain," "Derksen favoring" the *dʰer-* reading. — `[R63]`.

### D4. Greek *déndron* "tree" ← *dóru* — **formation uncertain**
- The reduplication *der-drew-om* → *déndron* is "highly atypical, so the formation must be regarded as uncertain"; Janda's alternative (*dem-* "house" + *drew-*) has a "weak" semantic basis. — `[R42]`. The English words built on *déndron* (*dendrite, rhododendron, philodendron, dendro-*) inherit this uncertainty at the deepest step only.

### D5. *trim* ← OE *trymman* "make firm" ← *trumaz* ← *deru-* — **connection uncertain**
- "the word is missing in Middle English after about 1250, which makes connection uncertain." — `[R15]`. The OE→PIE chain (*trum* "firm" ← *drumos* ← *deru-*) is itself fine `[R16]`; the doubt is whether modern *trim* continues OE *trymman*.

### D6. *tree* / *true* romantic gloss — **folk gloss, avoid**
- The popular saying that *true* "literally means 'as steadfast as a tree / true as an oak'" is a romanticized gloss. Sources support a *shared root* "be firm/solid" with *separate* specializations, not that *true* = "tree." State it as cognation, not identity. — `[R5]`,`[R2]`.

## §4 — Could-not-verify / cautions

These were checked and are recorded so they are **not** silently asserted:

- **PGmc *traują* (tray):** the Wiktionary Old English *trēġ* page stops at Proto-Germanic and gives **no** PIE root; etymonline supplies the *deru-* link and the form *\*traujam*. We cite etymonline `[R19]` and mark the deep link "per etymonline."
- **PGmc *triwwiz* vs *treuwaz* (true):** etymonline writes *\*treuwaz*; Wiktionary writes *\*triwwiz* (Gothic *triggws* implies a geminate *\*triggwaz*). Both are competing reconstructions of one etymon — attributed to their own sources, not merged.
- **Avestan form:** [R1]'s descendants list gives Iranian *\*dādráwah* → Avestan *dādruua*; the Sanskrit *dāru* page `[R51]` lists "Avestan *dāuru*" as a cognate. Spellings differ by page; we show *dādruua* with a note.
- **Latvian *darva*, Old Persian *dāru-*, a living Persian reflex, Hittite *taru-* "hard":** not confirmed in the consulted sources, so **omitted** (Lithuanian *derva* is kept as the Baltic representative).
- **Armenian *caṙ* (ծառ) "tree":** *not* in the *dóru* descendants list — it is generally a *different* root, so it is **not** linked here. The genuine Armenian reflexes are *tram*, *torg*, *tarr*.
- **Greek *Dryope*, *δροῦς*:** no Wiktionary entries (404); **omitted** rather than invented.
- **de Vaan p.184 verbatim:** quoted *via* Wiktionary's citation `[R26]`, not fetched directly from the book (PDF exceeded fetch limits). High-confidence but second-hand.
